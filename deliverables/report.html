One-liner: OUTSOURCE decides what deserves your attention, does the AI-eligible work, and prepares you for the exact moment only a human can unblock.

2. Motivation

Knowledge workers — MBA students, founders, operators — juggle hard deadlines, relationship obligations, career asks, and administrative walls in the same evening. Today’s coping tools are calendars, to-do lists, and generic chatbots. Calendars do not know goal importance. To-do lists do not compute opportunity cost. Chatbots draft text but happily “complete” tasks that actually require identity verification, presence, or signature.

The cost is not only hours. It is misallocated attention: polishing a low-leverage email while a fixed wedding window approaches, or pretending an insurance portal can be automated when the real blocker is a human identity check. OUTSOURCE exists for people who need a chief-of-staff pattern: understand goals, compare options, assign AI vs human honestly, execute what is executable, and stage the human intervention.

3. What is new here

Existing products nearby: Motion / Reclaim (calendar autopilot), ChatGPT / Copilot (general drafting), Notion AI (docs), personal CRM tools, and insurer apps themselves. Closest substitutes fail in different ways:

  • Calendar autopilots optimize free/busy, not life-goal opportunity cost.
  • General LLMs will invent completed actions (“I called the insurer”) unless constrained.
  • Task managers do not prepare private briefs + public ICS when a human gate appears.

OUTSOURCE’s twist: a strategy-aware execution office that treats human attention as the scarce resource. When work hits a barrier that AI cannot clear (demo: insurer identity verification), the system does not fake success — it prepares the intervention, schedules it, exports ICS without leaking sensitive details, and resumes after the user’s result.

Cost controls actually built: MAX_AGENT_CALLS (default 25) and MAX_WORKFLOW_COST_USD (default 2.00) raise BudgetExceeded before another live call. Demo mode avoids spend entirely. Prompt style asks for JSON-only structured outputs to limit completion length.

Tradeoff: aggressive truncation can hurt brief quality. We preferred spend caps + demo fixtures over silently degrading outputs. No fake “we saved 40%” claim without a paired A/B measurement in this submission.

6. Safety

  • Prompt injection: untrusted input is scanned for instruction-override patterns and wrapped with an explicit “data not commands” quarantine banner (core/safety.py).
  • Secret leakage: API-key-like strings are scrubbed to [REDACTED] before display/logs.
  • Agent malfunction: agents may not claim external side effects (emails sent, purchases). Insurer module is a labeled simulation.
  • Overspend: hard caps on calls and estimated USD in live mode.
  • Privacy: ICS public description excludes claim IDs and ID instructions; those stay in the private brief.

8. Limits + next steps

  • Live insurer, email, SMS, and purchases are not integrated.
  • Networking research and career-fork secondary demos are Planned.
  • Financial module is a deterministic teaching projection (taxes/retirement omitted).
  • Tokenomics in demo cannot show measured tokens — live keys required for that evidence.
  • Next month: real calendar OAuth, optional email draft→approve→send, richer evidence ledger UI, and a small pilot with 3–5 managers measuring weekly attention hours recovered.
